Who invented it?

Samuel Morse was a painter in America.

The story goes that in 1825 his wife got very sick while he was far away. The letter telling him took days to arrive, so he got home too late. He wanted news to travel faster.

In the 1830s he worked with his friend Alfred Vail to send messages down a wire, using electricity.

⚡

How did they make the code?

Electricity in a wire can only be on or off. So every letter became a pattern of short beeps (dots) and long beeps (dashes).

The story goes that Vail counted which letters printers used most. The most common letter, E, got the shortest code: just one dot. Rare letters like Q got long ones.

📡

The first message

On 24 May 1844, Morse sent a message from Washington to Baltimore, about 60 km away.

It said: "What hath God wrought". That means "Look what God has done!"

🚢

Where was it used?

  • Telegraph wires crossed whole countries. Later, cables ran under the ocean.
  • Ships used it by radio. In 1912 the Titanic sent SOS to call for help.
  • Soldiers and spies used it in both World Wars.

SOS is easy to remember: three dots, three dashes, three dots.

✈️

Is it still used today?

  • Planes: radio beacons near airports beep their short name in Morse, over and over, so pilots know they have the right one.
  • Radio fans (called "hams") all over the world still chat in Morse.
  • Navy ships flash messages to each other with big signal lamps.
  • Helping people: someone who can only move one finger, or blink, can type on a phone with Morse code.
🌟

Why learn it?

  • It works when nothing else does: with a torch, a whistle, tapping on a wall, or a very weak radio.
  • Anyone who knows SOS can call for help.
  • Learning by ear trains your listening and your memory.
  • It's a secret code for you and your friends!
  • Computers use two signals too, 0 and 1. Morse is a great first step into codes.

How to be a Morse agent

  1. Morse code is beeps. A short one is a dot. A long one is a dash. Each letter has its own tune.
  2. Learn it by ear. Don't count the dots. Listen to the tune, like a song.
  3. New here? Start with Boot Camp. It teaches dots, dashes and the key in about two minutes.
  4. Every mission adds a letter. Get 9 out of 10 right to unlock the next one.
  5. The key: tap it quick for a dot. Hold it for a dash. Stop, and the letter is sent.
  6. The lever on HQ makes it easier or harder. Rookie gives big gaps and lots of help.

Sound off? The lamp flashes with every beep. Tap 💡 to keep it on.
